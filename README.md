# Thermal Comfort Analysis

This repository contains a research project completed for the Fundamental Engineering Design course at Telkom University.

The project focuses on evaluating outdoor thermal comfort at several locations on campus using the Temperature Humidity Index (THI) and Physiological Equivalent Temperature (PET). The calculations were supported by Python, while the data were collected directly through field measurements.

## About the Project

This project was assigned by the course lecturer and completed by a team of three students.

Our goal was to understand how thermal conditions vary in different outdoor areas around Telkom University. To do this, we measured environmental conditions, processed the collected data, and analyzed the results using THI and PET.

Besides the technical analysis, we also documented the entire project in a research paper and presented the results through a project poster.

## My Role

I was involved in both the field work and the data analysis.

My responsibilities included:

- Collecting environmental data using two anemometers together with my teammates.
- Cleaning and organizing the measurement data before analysis.
- Calculating the Temperature Humidity Index (THI).
- Calculating the Physiological Equivalent Temperature (PET) using Python.
- Performing statistical calculations, including standard deviation, measurement uncertainty, and measurement error.
- Helping write the research paper and reviewing related references.

## Challenges

The biggest challenge was preparing the measurement data.

Because we used two different anemometers, the measurements were not always identical. Before starting the calculations, we had to compare the results from both devices and prepare a clean dataset that could be used for the analysis.

Learning how PET is calculated was also challenging because it was a new topic for me. Another part that took a lot of time was writing the research paper, especially searching for references and organizing the results into a clear technical report.

## What I Learned

This project gave me my first experience working on an engineering project that involved field measurements.

I learned that collecting data is only the beginning of the process. Before any analysis can be done, the data must be checked, cleaned, and organized carefully.

I also became more familiar with Python for engineering calculations, thermal comfort analysis, and basic statistical methods such as standard deviation and measurement uncertainty.

Working on the research paper also helped me improve my technical writing and literature review skills.
